## Summary

- The claim: "We developed a technique for live draft model training for speculative decoding, leading to a 20% increase in median accept rate, 100%+ on constrained traffic." In body: "Where rolled out, it has produced a median increase in accept rate of 20%, with some constrained traffic patterns seeing 100%+ increases." Restated later as "a median accept rate increase of 20%, with some constrained traffic patterns seeing a 100%+ increase in accept length". The baseline that this improves on is the static draft: "Draft models, such as EAGLE-3 and DFlash ... leading to 2-3x higher throughput and lower latency."
- The loop, what data: "The pipeline extracts hidden states directly from live inference and uses them to train the draft model on the fly, eliminating the need to store data offline." "the inference side sends unfiltered iteration data, which is only aggregated on the receiver side." So the training set is the production requests' own target-model hidden states, unfiltered, streamed per decode iteration.
- The loop, where: "Inference and training nodes run in parallel across a distributed cluster. Raw data is streamed from the inference process directly to the training aggregator ... Hidden states flow through CUDA-IPC double buffering into pageable memory via mmap ... The architecture scales horizontally across both data-parallel and context-parallel dimensions, so draft model training happens continuously alongside live inference without competing for resources." Training is on separate nodes, GPU-backed ("the added GPU and pinned memory usage is proportional to max_num_tokens_per_iter"), transferred by RDMA ("UCXX Python bindings to handle asynchronous RDMA transfers"), at "tens or hundreds of nodes".
- The loop, how often: NOT GIVEN. "continuously" and "on the fly" are the only cadence words; no refresh interval, no step count, no swap-in mechanism is stated.
- Why (the four bottlenecks it removes): "Storage overhead: ... A single sample on Kimi K2 can exceed 2GB, and full draft training requires millions of them." "Compute bottlenecks: Generating the hidden states required for draft model inputs can be prohibitively expensive". "Alignment drift: Fine-tuning or reinforcement learning (RL) on the base model often degrades the draft model's accept rate unless it is retrained alongside it." "Data compliance: Storing data for offline training can be difficult in zero data retention (ZDR) environments."
- Serving-side overhead controls: "we offload all network communication and data buffering to a dedicated background process. Paired with careful CUDA event synchronization on the overlap scheduler loop"; memory "is proportional to max_num_tokens_per_iter, not max_sequence_length". Training side: "completely decoupled the data loaders from the core training loop", "mmap-backed arrays to buffer training data directly into paged memory", "the full request is assembled in pageable memory and only pinned just before it enters the training loop". Concurrency via Trio "guest loop mode with PyTorch synchronization points such as torch.cuda.synchronize() to run an async loop without creating new threads, thereby minimizing GIL contention."
- Secondary claim, unquantified: "It has significantly reduced the time required to train draft models for new base models, while also allowing those models to continuously adapt to custom traffic."

## Relevance to local-maxxing

Speculative decoding is the one lever on the bytes-per-token ledger that does not touch the target's bytes at all: each verify step reads the target's full weights once and amortizes them over 1+accepted tokens, so accept rate IS the bytes-per-token divisor. This blog is evidence that the divisor is not fixed by the draft's training set but drifts with traffic, and that re-aligning the draft on the served distribution moves it by +20% median / +100% on narrow traffic. The town's traffic is exactly the "constrained traffic pattern" case: kids and parents emit graph-node prose, YAML frontmatter, and the same skill preambles every turn, which is where the 100%+ end of the range lives. The blog's loop (target hidden states streamed off the serving box, draft trained elsewhere) maps directly onto the cluster's shape: local-town serves (gpu-8g), and the draft head (1-5% of target per the sibling baseten-eagle3-heads digest; ~16 MB for one Qwen3-0.6B layer) is small enough that its gradient step fits on a CPU (Ryzen 3700X 16t, or even the swarm box's 4 Ampere cores) if the hidden states are shipped over the gigabit LAN rather than RDMA. The blog does not say CPU training is possible; it says the training is on GPU nodes. Whether a head-sized draft trains fast enough on CPU to track drift is the open question the town has to measure. For the looped transformer: a depth-recurrent target makes the "hidden state" the blog extracts a per-loop-iteration quantity, so the same stream could feed early-exit calibration and draft training from one tap; for the flip/SNN thread the analogue is the draft as a cheap oscillator predicting which bits will flip, verified by the full cascade. Stockpile note: the loop is contingent on a serving engine that exposes hidden states per iteration (llama.cpp does not, out of the box; SGLang/SpecForge does), which is a harness cost, "never the model always the harness".

## Idea seed

- **slug**: lm-live-draft-cpu-refit
- **title**: Refit the draft head on the town's own traffic, CPU-side, while the GPU serves
- **lever**: Retraining the draft on served-traffic hidden states raised median accept rate +20% (100%+ on constrained traffic) over a static draft, and accept rate is the divisor on target bytes-per-token, so the same ~600 MB/verify-step (Qwen3-0.6B Q8) or ~2.0-2.5 GB (Qwen3.5-4B Q4) is spread over 1.2x as many tokens for zero extra target bytes.
- **buys**: local-town serves the target with a static EAGLE-3 head while the Ryzen's 16 threads (or the swarm box's 4) continuously refit the ~16-100 MB head from hidden states tapped off the serving process and shipped over the gigabit LAN, swapping the head in at intervals; the town's narrow kid/parent prose is the "constrained traffic" regime where the blog reports the 100%+ end, so decode tok/s on the swarm box's 6.9 tok/s 4B could plausibly move ~+20% over whatever the static head gives.
- **first_falsifier**: Log accepted-tokens-per-verify-step on 2,000 real town requests with a static head, refit the head for one CPU hour on the hidden states of the first 1,000, replay the second 1,000, and if accept length rises less than +10% (half the blog's median) the drift-refit lever is dead on our traffic.
- **cheapest_test_on_our_iron**: On local-town, serve Qwen3-0.6B (or Qwen3-4B) with SGLang + a public EAGLE-3 head, dump per-iteration hidden states to an mmap file, run one SpecForge-style head training epoch on the Ryzen CPU only (torch, no CUDA) against ~1k town transcripts, and compare accept length before/after in a replay; roughly 2-4 wall-clock hours, $0 (own hardware), the only unknown being whether a CPU epoch finishes inside the hour.

1. **"1.2x as many tokens" (idea lever) is not in the source and does not follow from it.** The blog's +20% is a relative change in "accept rate" (para 3) and, in its own restatement, in "accept length" — two different quantities the blog conflates and never gives in absolute terms, with no draft length. Accepted tokens per verify step is a nonlinear function of per-position accept rate (for draft length g and per-position rate a it is (1 - a^(g+1))/(1 - a)), so +20% in rate is not +20% in tokens per step; and the ledger's divisor is 1 + accepted length per target pass, not accept rate (the Relevance paragraph's "accept rate IS the bytes-per-token divisor" is loose). Correction: the source supports only "median +20% in the blog's accept metric, on Baseten production traffic"; any token-per-step multiplier is unmeasured.
2. **The Town-side derivation in Numbers (2.0x -> 2.4x; 6.9 -> 8.3 tok/s; 13.8 -> 16.5 tok/s) compounds two unsupported assumptions.** It multiplies a speedup by an accept-rate ratio (wrong functional form, see 1) and takes a 2x static-draft speedup on the swarm box as its baseline, whereas the town's own H7 (trove-survey-2026-09-14, EXP1) predicts <= 1.3x on prose with a model draft on 4 ARM cores. The blog's 2-3x is GPU serving of large models. Correction: no tok/s figure exists for our iron until H7 runs; delete the chain or label every link as a guess.
3. **"The town's narrow kid/parent prose is the 'constrained traffic' regime where the blog reports the 100%+ end" is the reader's gloss, not the source's.** The blog never defines "constrained traffic patterns" (domain-narrow? rate-limited? low-entropy?) and gives no example traffic.
