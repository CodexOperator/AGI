## Summary

- The head: "the draft head is just a single transformer decoder layer, typically only 1-5% of the target model's parameters, so training is fast and resource-efficient." The target model "verifies those predictions in a single forward pass"; "verified tokens are identical to what the target model would have generated."
- The measured gain: "At Baseten, we've found 1.5-2.5x latency improvements on models trained with EAGLE heads. The target model used was a Qwen3-4B." The blog discounts the papers: "While EAGLE papers report up to 4-6x speedups in benchmarks, some of that gain comes from differences in serving frameworks rather than the draft head alone. In production, we typically observe 1.5-2.5x latency improvements attributable to the EAGLE head itself."
- Who benefits: "Memory-bound workloads benefit most: if your inference is bottlenecked by memory bandwidth (common for long-context or single-batch serving), EAGLE heads can cut latency significantly." "EAGLE works best when your workload is latency-sensitive but not heavily batched."
- Training knobs: TTT-length "Recommended: 7-9" ("how many tokens the EAGLE head generates using its own prior predictions as context during training"); inference draft tokens "Recommended: 3-4" ("Going higher (e.g., 8) rarely helps"); AdamW with LR by target size: "Small (~3-7B) 1e-4 / Medium (~7-20B) 5e-5 / Large (20B+) 2e-5".
- Data: "Dataset quality is the most important factor"; for models "~20B or less": "Generic (conversations, creative writing) 200k-300k samples", "Specialized (structured output, JSON, specific formats) ~100k samples"; large models ~500k generic / ~100k specialized; "Aim for 1k-2k total tokens per sample (prompt + completion combined)." The golden rule: "regenerate outputs with the target model" (e.g. "start from a dataset like UltraChat-200k and regenerate all completions using your target model").
- Convergence signals: loss shows "a sharp drop in the first 10-20% of steps, then a slow taper"; "Accuracy should climb steeply and stabilize at 70-80%". Sampling: "EAGLE heads perform best under greedy decoding (temperature=0)"; "The EAGLE paper reports roughly a 15-25% reduction in speedup at temperature=1 vs. temperature=0."
- Tooling: NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer, sgl-project/SpecForge, torchspec-project/TorchSpec "all work fine"; heads are "saved to Hugging Face and can be referenced by any serving framework that supports EAGLE-3". A "Part 2" on "DFlash" and scaling EAGLE training is promised, not published in this page.

## Relevance to local-maxxing

The blog's one sentence that matters to the ledger is "memory-bound workloads benefit most ... single-batch serving" — that is exactly the swarm box (Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0 at 34-45 tok/s = copy bandwidth; Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M at 6.9 tok/s). Speculative decoding is the only lever in the treasury that does not change the target's bytes at all: it amortises one full weight read over 1+accepted tokens, so bytes-touched-per-emitted-token falls by the accepted length while the head adds a 2.6% layer plus (the trap for a 0.6B target) an LM-head read that is 10x the head layer unless the draft vocab is cut. The blog's 1.5-2.5x at 3-4 drafts on a 4B target is therefore the honest ceiling for the 4B box, not 4-6x. Two thread ties: (1) an EAGLE-3 head trained at TTT-length 7-9 is literally a one-layer transformer run recurrently on its own outputs — the smallest instance of the depth-recurrent looped transformer, and a head that is a loop step of the target is the natural draft for a looped target; (2) verification makes a lossy drafter lossless, which is the harness the E3 byte-neuron LUT / flip-mode Kuramoto thread has been missing — a bit-flip cascade that is right 70% of the time is useless as a model and useful as a drafter. The training cost is data, not the head: 100k specialised samples x 1-2k tokens = 100-200M target-regenerated tokens, which at 6.9 tok/s single-stream is years, so regeneration must be batched on local-town (gpu-8g: Qwen3-0.6B bf16 = ~1.2 GB fits with room for the head and 3 hidden-state taps; Qwen3-4B bf16 = ~8 GB does not fit for a training-time target forward without 8-bit weights or CPU offload) — the blog is silent on CPU/8 GB training, so that is an untested claim of ours, and the first cost to measure.

## Idea seed

- slug: lm-eagle3-head-per-byte
- title: One-layer draft head as the bandwidth amortiser: accepted-length x fewer bytes per token
- lever: Attach a single-decoder-layer EAGLE-3 head (~2.6% of Qwen3-0.6B = ~16 MB at Q8, ~101M params for Qwen3-4B) trained on target-regenerated data so that 3-4 drafted tokens are verified in one target pass, cutting bytes touched per emitted token by the accepted length (blog: 1.5-2.5x latency on Qwen3-4B, memory-bound single-batch).
- buys: On the swarm box's Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M at 6.9 tok/s a 1.5-2.5x amortisation is 10-17 tok/s for zero change to the target's weights or quality; the same verify-loop is the harness that turns a lossy E3/flip-mode drafter into a lossless one, and the head itself is a one-layer looped transformer trained at TTT 7-9 to eat its own outputs.
- first_falsifier: Run llama.cpp draft-model speculative decoding on the swarm box (Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0 drafting for a same-vocab Qwen3 4B Q4_K_M, --draft-max 4, temperature 0) and measure mean accepted length and tok/s; if accepted length is under ~1.5 or tok/s does not clear 6.9, no head that is 10x smaller than that drafter will do better on this iron and the idea dies without training anything.
- cheapest_test_on_our_iron: swarm box, llama-speculative with the resident 4B Q4_K_M as target and Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0 as draft over 20 prompts of the town's own kid transcripts, ~1 hour wall-clock, $0, reporting accepted length, tok/s and bytes-per-token vs the 6.9 tok/s baseline.

### Errors, each with the correction

1. **The first_falsifier and cheapest_test cannot run on this box as written.** They pair "Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0 drafting for a same-vocab Qwen3 4B Q4_K_M". There is no Qwen3-4B GGUF resident; the resident 4B is `Qwen3.5-4B-Q4_K_M.gguf` (2,740,937,888 B), whose config.json says `vocab_size: 248320`, while Qwen3-0.6B has `vocab_size: 151936`. llama.cpp's `common_speculative_are_compatible` (common/speculative.cpp L30, L108) refuses a draft whose vocab differs from the target's by more than `SPEC_VOCAB_MAX_SIZE_DIFFERENCE 128` tokens. The vocabs differ by 96,384. **Correction:** the resident same-vocab draft is `Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q8_0.gguf` (811,843,840 B; config.json `vocab_size: 248320`, same tokenizer family).
2. **Wrong binary and a removed flag.** No `llama-speculative` binary exists here (`/home/ubuntu/src/llama.cpp/build/bin/` has `llama-server`, `llama-cli`, `llama-bench`), and `llama-server --help` on that build prints for `--draft-max`: "the argument has been removed. use --spec-draft-n-max". **Correction:** `llama-server -m <target> -md <draft> --spec-type draft-simple --spec-draft-n-max 4` (default 3).
3. **The falsifier's inference is inverted.** "if accepted length is under ~1.5 ... no head that is 10x smaller than that drafter will do better" — a smaller drafter costs *fewer* bytes per draft token, so it can win where a big standalone drafter loses; and llama.cpp's docs/speculative.md states an EAGLE-3 head "reaches higher acceptance than a standalone draft model of the same size". On this box a Qwen3.5-0.8B Q8 draft step reads ~812 MB = 30% of the 2.74 GB target file, so 4 drafts read 1.2 target-steps of bytes *before* the verify; a negative result there says nothing about a 233 MB head. The "~1.5" threshold is derived from nothing in the source. **Correction:** the falsifier that actually kills the idea is the verify cost — measure a 4-5-token batch forward vs a 1-token step on 4 cores (`llama-bench` pp vs tg); if the batch costs >= ~2.5x a single step, the box is compute-bound at verify and no drafter at any acceptance reaches 1.5x.
4. **"10-17 tok/s" is unsupported for this iron.** The blog's 1.5-2.5x is a GPU serving measurement (hardware, batch, framework unstated) and the blog never ties it to "3-4 drafts" as the Relevance section claims ("The blog's 1.5-2.5x at 3-4 drafts"). The only measured accept length / speedup for the town's actual target is the head card (yuyijiong/Qwen3.5-4B-Eagle3, SGLang, 1 GPU, `--spec-topk 1`): temp 0, `spec-num-steps` 3 -> mean accept length 2.76 / speedup 1.91x; 7 -> 3.50 / 2.10x; 15 -> 3.66 / 1.70x; temp 1.0, steps 3 -> 2.29 / 1.53x. Nothing exists for a 4-core ARM CPU; that is what the test measures.
5. **"The head itself is a one-layer looped transformer trained at TTT 7-9 to eat its own outputs" is the reader's, not the source's.** The blog defines TTT-length as "how many tokens the EAGLE head generates using its own prior predictions as context during training" — autoregression over sequence positions, exactly what any decoder does at inference; the word "looped", "recurrent" or "depth" never appears. The head is applied once per drafted token, not repeatedly to the same hidden state. **Correction:** label the looped-transformer tie as a town analogy; the source supports only "a single transformer decoder layer".
6. **"Verification makes a lossy E3/flip-mode drafter lossless" is not this source's claim.** The blog's lossless statement is about EAGLE-3 verified tokens. An E3 LUT / flip-mode cascade would not be an EAGLE-3 head: EAGLE-3 reads target hidden states (the head's config.json `eagle_aux_hidden_state_layer_ids: [1, 15, 28]`; llama.cpp L473 throws unless the draft declares exactly 3 extract layers). The general property belongs to standard draft-model speculative decoding (llama.cpp `draft-simple`), and holds only with a proper verify. **Correction:** keep the thread tie, attribute it to speculative decoding in general and route it through `draft-simple`, not through this blog.
7. **"32k reduced draft vocab" and "~49 MB (8%)" have no source.** The real Qwen3.5-4B head (config.json) uses `draft_vocab_size: 50000`; its lm_head is 50,000 x 2,560 = 128M params = 55% of the head. **Correction:** replace 32k with 50k and recompute.
8. **The one-layer head is undercounted ~2x and the 4B arithmetic is for the wrong model.** A real EAGLE-3 head is not a bare decoder layer: the Qwen3.5-4B head's safetensors is 466,385,472 B bf16 = 233.2M params (fc 7,680->2,560 = 19.7M; attention on a 5,120-wide [embed;hidden] input with 16/4 heads x head_dim 160 = 26.2M; MLP 3 x 2,560 x 7,680 = 59.0M; lm_head 128M; embed_tokens shared with the target, not stored). That is ~5.8% of the target, above the reader's "2.5%" and at the edge of the blog's "1-5%", and 233 MB at Q8 = 8.5% of the resident 2.74 GB Q4_K_M file per draft token. The digest's "Qwen3-4B one layer = ~100.9M (36 layers, 32/8 heads, intermediate 9728)" is correct for Qwen3-4B but the box runs Qwen3.5-4B (32 layers of which 24 `linear_attention` and 8 `full_attention`, 16/4 heads x head_dim 256, intermediate 9216, `vocab_size 248320`) — a different architecture, so none of the "4B box" per-layer or LM-head (389M) figures apply to it (Qwen3.5-4B LM head = 248,320 x 2,560 = 636M).
9. **"~2.0-2.5 GB/token" for the 4B Q4 understates the resident file** (2,740,937,888 B = 2.74 GB, 10-35% more). "~600 MB" for 0.6B Q8 is fine (file 639,447,744 B).
10. **"at 6.9 tok/s single-stream is years"** — 100-200M tokens / 6.9 tok/s = 168-336 days, under a year. And the premise is moot: a trained head for the town's target already exists (item 4), so the first test needs zero training tokens.
11. **The temperature number is second-hand.** The blog attributes "15-25% reduction in speedup at temperature=1" to "The EAGLE paper"; the Numbers list cites it as "(blog, Sampling parameters)". Correction: "(blog, citing the EAGLE paper)".
12. **What the reader missed that changes the test entirely:** the local llama.cpp build (093a2f8) already supports `--spec-type draft-eagle3` (tools/server/README.md L271) and `convert_hf_to_gguf.py --target-model-dir` converts a SpecForge `LlamaForCausalLMEagle3` checkpoint (docs/speculative.md L24-32); the head's architecture is exactly `LlamaForCausalLMEagle3`. The blog's own recipe was followed by that head (card: LR 1e-4, TTT 7, epochs 2, max seq 2048, "Top-50k tokens from UltraChat"), so the blog is corroborated by a third party on the town's model — and the town can measure an EAGLE-3 head on its iron this week without local-town.
