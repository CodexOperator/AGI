## Summary (measured lines, all from the v2 PDF text)

1. Mechanism: standard speculative decoding (draft proposes k tokens, target verifies k+1 in one parallel pass, rejection-sampling keeps the target's exact distribution), plus **online knowledge distillation of the draft from the target's own verification logits**. "If the student proposes incorrect tokens, both the draft and target distributions are stored in a buffer. Once the buffer exceeds a specified threshold, the draft model is updated by calculating the loss between the draft and target distributions" (Fig. 1 caption). The training signal is free: "the speculative decoding process inherently identifies the inaccuracies of the small draft LLM and offers correct solutions for these inaccuracies ... at no additional cost" (§3).
2. What runs where / how often (Algorithm 1, §4.2.1): per request, record `(error index, target logits at error index)` pairs into a temporary buffer R, append `(x, R)` to replay buffer Q; "OSD updates the draft model every I iteration, with I being a dynamically adjustable parameter"; "For all online experiments, we fix the update interval I at 8" (§5); proposals k=5 ("we set the number of proposed tokens to 5"); "we refrain from evicting any pairs and release Q after each model update". Hardware: the update is meant to consume the serving cluster's idle accelerators ("spare flops"), "opportunistically only when the service traffic is not on spike"; and A.2: "the processes of updating and inference can even be executed concurrently on separate devices." **The paper never names a CPU** — its spare FLOPs are GPU FLOPs sitting idle because decode is HBM-bandwidth-bound ("generating each word requires loading the model weights from HBM to SRAM as well as reading the KV cache").
3. Model pairs and sizes (§5): target Vicuna-7B with draft LLaMA-160M; target FLAN-T5-XL (3B) with draft T5-Small (80M). Four tasks: Spider (text-to-SQL), Gsm8k, Code-search-Python, Alpaca-finance. Loss: forward KL with **teacher sampling** wins ("the teacher sampling method outperforms others"); RKL/JSD "either paralleled or underperformed".
4. Acceptance rate (Table 1, offline distillation, 2 epochs, Original -> TF): Vicuna-7B Spider 0.28 -> 0.76, Gsm8k 0.58 -> 0.75, Code-search-Python 0.38 -> 0.65, Alpaca-finance 0.57 -> 0.67; FLAN-T5-XL Spider 0.13 -> 0.78, Gsm8k 0.29 -> 0.62, Code-search-Python 0.28 -> 0.81, Alpaca-finance 0.39 -> 0.63. Abstract: "increase in the token acceptance rate by 0.1 to 0.65, which translates into 1.22x to 3.06x latency reduction".
5. Latency (§4.2.2): speedup of OSD over static speculative decoding is (1+α2+...+α2^k)/(1+α1+...+α1^k); measured factors "for Vicuna-7B (LLaMA-160M as the draft model) by factors of 2.42x, 1.43x, 1.64x, and 1.22x. Similarly, for Flan-T5-XL 3B (T5-small 80M as the draft model), the speedup enhancements are 3.06x, 1.76x, 2.72x, and 1.55x" — these are **theoretical** ("1.2-3.1x reduction in latency theoretically", §1 contributions), computed from α, not wall-clock.
6. FLOPs (A.3): training ≈ 6 FLOPs/param/token vs 2 for inference; target-vs-draft FLOPs ratio "(5+1)x7B / (5+3x3)x160M = 18.75" at α=0.71 and "(5+1)x3B / (5+3x4.3)x80M = 12.6" at α=0.76; the LMSYS-Chat-1M trace ("125 days with 1000,000 requests, averaging less than 2,000 tokens per request") served by a 30B model needs "5.5 x 10^9 FLOPs or 5.5 GFLOPs" per second against "8 x 312 TFLOPs" — "computational utilization is under 1 percent".
7. Online behaviour (§5.2, Figs. 3-5): α measured as moving average over the last 50 (or 100) requests; online curves start below the 10%-data offline-distilled baseline then "rise swiftly" to match "Offline Test Alphas" within ~2-8K records; on distribution shifts (2K-record blocks) "OSD's alpha value dips notably at distribution boundaries, especially around 2K, 4K, and 6K records" and rebounds; on real LMSYS-chat conversations per-language drafts "enhances rates by 0.1 to 0.2, even with under 2K data points"; topic-clustered English drafts reach "above 0.6 across topics, with Social and Computer discussions peaking near 0.8". A.4: alternating Gsm8k then Alpaca-finance updates showed the Gsm8k rate "remains consistent, suggesting that the draft model retains its learned knowledge without showing signs of forgetting."
8. Qualitative (§5.3): the top-100 target tokens "account for 72.2% of all appearances, following a power-law distribution"; distillation raises both precision and recall on those (Fig. 6); the learned tokens are domain tokens (SELECT/WHERE for Spider, <<, >>, =, + for Gsm8k).

## Relevance to local-maxxing

- **Bytes-per-token ledger.** Speculative decoding is the direct answer to the swarm box's regime: decode is bandwidth-bound, so each accepted draft token replaces one full target weight sweep with one draft sweep, and the verify pass amortizes one target sweep over a+1 tokens. Everything hangs on α and on c; the paper's Eq. 2 and Fig. 2 say plainly that low α makes speculation *slower* than plain decode. OSD's whole contribution is that α is not fixed — it can be bought online, from logits the target computes anyway, at roughly 1/12-1/19 of the target's FLOPs. For the town that is a byte-lever: every +0.1 of α at k=5 is a measurable drop in target sweeps per token.
- **The owner's CPU question, answered honestly.** The paper places the draft update on idle *GPU* FLOPs, and its only concession to heterogeneity is one sentence in A.2 ("executed concurrently on separate devices"). Nothing in it is about CPUs. But the split it describes maps cleanly onto our cluster: the verification logits are the only thing the trainer needs, they are a byte-stream, and the update happens every I=8 requests — coarse enough to ship over a LAN. On the swarm box itself the caveat is sharper than the paper's: the A1 is bandwidth-bound, so a backward pass on the draft competes with decode for the *same* memory bus, not for idle ALUs — "spare FLOPs" on a 4-core CPU are not free the way idle A100 tensor cores are. The clean version is target on local-town (2070S), draft training on the 3700X CPU or on the GPU between bursts, and the *draft itself* on the swarm box only if the per-step round trip can be batched. Also: k+1-token verification "takes roughly the same amount of time as generating a single token" is a GPU assumption; on the A1 a 6-row matmul may not be free.
- **Looped transformer / flip-SNN thread.** A depth-recurrent target is the natural OSD host: fewer loops = a draft, more loops = the target, one weight set, and the "draft" distills toward the deeper unroll of itself — the acceptance signal is then a per-token exit criterion (this is the early-exit / self-speculation family, not this paper, but this paper supplies the online-distillation loop that keeps the shallow pass aligned as the workload drifts). For the byte-neuron / digital-Kuramoto line, the reusable piece is the *replay buffer of (error index, target logits)* — a compact, free supervision stream any cheap proposer (LUT cascade, oscillator ensemble) can be fit against, and α is the one scalar that grades every such proposer against the same target.
- **Powering our own kids.** The town's kid traffic is narrow (one graph, one skill, a few node schemas) — exactly the "relatively simple" query distribution the paper exploits; their 72.2%-in-100-tokens power law is what lets a tiny draft learn the workload in "under 2K data points". A draft distilled on kid transcripts is a cheap, self-improving front end for a 4B target, and the same buffer is a free dataset for any later distillation.

## Idea seed

- **slug:** `lm-online-draft-distillation-cpu-split`
- **title:** Online draft distillation from verify logits, trainer split off the decode box
- **lever:** Distill the draft online from the target's verification logits every I=8 requests (forward KL, teacher-sampled), which in the paper lifted acceptance by +0.1 to +0.65 and cut latency 1.22-3.06x at 1/12-1/19 of the target's FLOPs; on our ledger each +0.1 α at k=5 removes a fraction of full target weight sweeps (~2.5 GB for Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M) in favour of draft sweeps (~0.6 GB for Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0).
- **buys:** A 4B-class target on the swarm box that decodes closer to the 0.6B's 34-45 tok/s on kid traffic, with the draft getting better the longer the town runs, and the trainer (the only part that needs FLOPs) parked on local-town's 3700X/2070S while the decode box just streams `(error index, target logits)` pairs over the LAN/WireGuard once every 8 requests.
- **first_falsifier:** On the A1, time one Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M forward over a 6-token batch versus 1 token; if the batch-6 verify costs more than ~1.6x a single step, or the measured c (0.6B step / 4B step) puts Eq. 2's break-even α above ~0.75 at k=5, speculation cannot pay on this box no matter how good the draft gets, and the idea dies before any distillation.
- **cheapest_test_on_our_iron:** On the swarm box, run llama.cpp `llama-speculative` with Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M target and Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0 draft (k=5) over ~200 kid prompts, logging per-step accepted count, draft-step and verify-step wall time — about 1-2 hours, $0 — to get baseline α, c and the actual tok/s against the 6.9 tok/s plain-decode reference; a positive result then gates a 1-epoch LoRA forward-KL distillation of the 0.6B on the logged target logits on local-town (torch venv, 2070S) and a re-measure of α.

**Errors and unsupported numbers (digest, then idea):**

1. **Stale headline, not flagged as superseded.** The digest reports v2's theoretical "1.22x to 3.06x". The current arXiv version (v4, 10 Jun 2024, 16 pp) rewrote the abstract to "increase in the token acceptance rate by 0.1 to 0.65, bringing 1.42x to 2.17x latency reduction" (v4 L43) and added measured A100-80G, batch-1, k=8 numbers (v4 Table 2/7): Vicuna-7B + LLaMA-160M (c=0.13) measured 0.95x at α=0.5, 1.17x at 0.6, 1.3x at 0.7, 1.68x at 0.8, 2.55x at 0.9, versus theoretical 0.98x/1.21x/1.57x/2.12x/3.00x at the same α (v4 L1069-1073); per-dataset measured 1.63x/1.60x/1.47x/1.42x/1.36x for the 160M/7B pair (v4 L554). So the authors themselves withdrew 3.06x; measured speedup runs 15-25% under Eq. 2 at every α and is <1 at α=0.5. The idea's lever quotes the withdrawn number.
2. **"within ~2-8K records" (summary bullet 6) is not in the source.** The paper says only that rates "rise swiftly" and "can achieve comparable results" (L575-579); 2-8K is the reader eyeballing Fig. 3's x-axes (0-6K and 0-8K, L568-569). Unsupported number.
3. **"idle GPU FLOPs" is an inference.** The paper says "spare flops ... available in a typical LLM serving cluster" (L62-63) and grounds the bandwidth argument in HBM (L85, L159); it never says the update runs on a GPU, nor on anything else. Reasonable, but the reader's words.
4. **"at 1/12-1/19 of the target's FLOPs" (relevance + idea lever) misattributes the ratio.** 18.75 and 12.6 are (k+1)·Fp / (k+3a)·Fq (L923-926): target inference over the **whole draft budget, draft inference k tokens plus training 3a**. The trainer alone is 3a/(k+3a) of that — 9/14 ≈ 64% for the Vicuna pair — so "distillation costs 1/19 of the target" overstates the paper by ~1.5x. Corrected: draft inference + online update together cost ~1/19 (7B pair) and ~1/13 (3B pair) of the target's inference FLOPs.
5. **Idea "~2.5 GB for Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M"** — the file on the box is 2,740,937,888 B = 2.74 GB. "~0.6 GB for Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0" is 639,447,744 B = 0.64 GB (fine).
6. **The cheapest test as written cannot run: Qwen3-0.6B cannot draft for Qwen3.5-4B in llama.cpp.** GGUF metadata: Qwen3.5-4B vocab 248,320 tokens, `tokenizer.ggml.pre=qwen35`; Qwen3-0.6B vocab 151,936, `pre=qwen2`. `common/speculative.cpp` `common_speculative_are_compatible` rejects a draft whose vocab size differs by more than `SPEC_VOCAB_MAX_SIZE_DIFFERENCE 128` (L30, L108); the difference here is 96,384. The only compatible draft in `~/.cache/lm-models/` is **Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q8_0** (811,843,840 B = 0.81 GB, identical token list). This also voids the "buys" phrasing "closer to the 0.6B's 34-45 tok/s" — the 0.6B is not in the loop; the 0.8B's tok/s on this box is unmeasured.
7. **`llama-speculative` is not built on the box.** `build/bin/` holds `llama-bench`, `llama-cli`, `llama-perplexity`, `llama-server` only. `llama-server` does expose `-md/--spec-draft-model`, `--spec-type draft-simple`, `--spec-draft-n-max N` (default 3, so k=5 must be set explicitly), `--spec-draft-threads`, and reports `draft_n`/`draft_n_accepted` in timings — use that, or build `tools/speculative-simple`.
8. **First-falsifier thresholds "~1.6x" and "~0.75" are the reader's, not the source's, and 0.75 is loose.** Eq. 2 (L224) with c taken as the draft/target bytes ratio on a bandwidth-bound box — 0.81/2.74 = 0.30 for the 0.8B draft — gives break-even at k=5 of α ≈ 0.64; with the (incompatible) 0.6B, c=0.23 → α ≈ 0.56. Corrected falsifier: measure c and the batch-6 verify cost directly and plug them in; if the break-even α exceeds what plain kid traffic gives with an untrained 0.8B draft by more than the paper's typical online gain (+0.1..0.2 on real chat traffic, L674), the idea dies.
9. **"1-epoch LoRA forward-KL distillation" is not the paper's mechanism.** OSD updates the full draft (Algorithm 1 L376-379, "update the draft model"); LoRA is never mentioned. Allowed as a town adaptation but must not be attributed to the source.
10. **The online half needs a logits tap the town does not have.** Algorithm 1 stores the target's full distribution `p(y|x, y<a+1)` at each error index (L373). Stock `llama-server` emits top-`n_probs` only, and a full fp16 row over a 248,320 vocab is 496,640 B per error position — at α=0.6 that is ~0.2 MB per generated token to ship over WireGuard, not the "byte-stream once every 8 requests" the idea implies (the *update* is every 8 requests; the *logging* is per token). A top-k-truncated row (the paper's own 72.2%-in-100-tokens observation, L686, argues top-100..1000 suffices) is the practical form, and is a deviation from the paper to be measured, not assumed.
11. **"The draft improving the longer the town runs"** — the paper's evidence is drift-adaptation with dips at every boundary (L657-660) and no forgetting in one 2-dataset mix (A.4); "monotonically better" is not shown.

**What survives:** the mechanism (verify logits at rejection points are free supervision; periodic draft update; α as the single scalar), the FLOPs argument (with the ratio corrected per item 4), the A.2 "separate devices" split, and the town's bandwidth reading of Eq. 2. The idea survives with the draft swapped to Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q8_0, the lever quoting v4's measured 1.42-2.17x instead of v2's theoretical 3.06x, the test rerouted through `llama-server -md`, and the online stage gated on a top-k logits tap.
