"I was thinking we could combine gpu capacity with all the cpus we have available across the cluster (m3 is not available). … I was thinking the CPUs could somehow parallelize the workloads of decoding training or decoding speculation or something like it" (H1, 2026-09-16)

## Actionable claims
1. CEILING (local-town, ~20 min after ORDER 4's server lands, $0 + 0.81 GB): PEARL's bound (parallel speedup ≤ t_target/t_draft) puts the CPU-draft/GPU-verify split under 2× — tg(Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q8_0, -ngl 0, 16 threads) < 2 × tg(9B, -ngl 99) — and llama.cpp's sequential draft-simple under break-even (Eq. 2, k=5: c ≥ 0.5 needs α ≥ 0.78); both models in VRAM (bytes 0.81/5.68 → c ≈ 0.14, break-even α ≈ 0.42, E(0.7) ≈ 1.7×) is the only pairing in range. FALSIFIER: CPU draft ≥ 2× faster per step than the GPU target AND -ngld 0 speculation ≥ 1.2× plain 9B tok/s on 20 kid prompts at temp 0.
2. VERIFY GATE (swarm box, 5 min, $0): the 6-token verify batch of Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M costs 1.6-2.0× a decode step (compute floor 1.61), so with c = 0.21 Eq. 2 breaks even at α = 0.65-0.72 — inside H7's 55-75% — i.e. speculation on the A1 is ≤ ~1.2× and no off-box CPU lowers v (verification is one batched forward on the target's box). FALSIFIER: batch-6 ≥ 2.5× (break-even α ≥ 0.78: the A1 is target-only, its decode-parallel half of H1 is dead) or ≤ 1.2× (bandwidth-bound after all: break-even α ≈ 0.55, H7's cap is too pessimistic).
3. CPU TRAINER (local-town CPU, ≤ 20 min, $0 + 1.6 GB): the one speculation job a cluster CPU can take off the decode path is OSD's draft update (Algorithm 1, every I=8 requests, from (error index, target logits) pairs); the 3700X sustains ≥ 35 tok/s of fp32 forward-KL training on Qwen3.5-0.8B at seq 512 with RSS ≤ 13 GB → a 1M-token refit in ≤ 8 h, one night, GPU untouched; the tap is llama-server n_probs=100 (800 B/token, 24 KB/s at 30 tok/s, WAN-safe) not full rows (497 KB/token, 15 MB/s, LAN-only). FALSIFIER: < 10 tok/s or RSS > 13 GB (only LoRA / a head-sized draft stays CPU-trainable; the trainer is the GPU with the target unloaded), or median top-100 mass of the 9B on 200 kid positions < 0.9.

## Dead ends (the evidence already argues against)
- Draft on encryption-town or across the WAN: PEARL's ceiling t_target/t_draft (paper pairs 2.7-5.4×) is ≤ ~1 when a 2011 i5 drafts for a ≥ 30 tok/s GPU 9B step (the faster A1 already needs 31 ms per 0.8B step); PEARL's own comm is 0.2-0.3 ms on one node, the WAN RTT is unmeasured and paid once per block.
- CPUs "parallelizing verification": one batched target forward; on the A1 the 4B's 39 ms/token compute floor makes batch-6 ≥ 1.6× a step and nothing off-box lowers it.
- Qwen3-0.6B as draft for any Qwen3.5 target: vocab Δ 96,384 > 128 — only Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q8_0 (811,843,840 B) pairs; llama-speculative is not built, --draft-max is removed.
- Impulse/reward-only drafter training (the flip/SNN bridge as stated): DVI PG-only 0.035 / CE-only 0.039 MAT vs KL-only 1.933; E3 predicts 1 bit/tick from an 8-bit window, not a 248,320-way token.
- Same-GPU parallel draft/verify (PEARL co-location): "significant slowdowns", 89-99% retained only with multi-GPU pipeline parallel; llama.cpp 093a2f8 has no overlap path at all.
- Paper speedups as targets: OSD v4 1.42-2.17× (A100, k=8; 0.95× at α=0.5), PEARL 2.48-4.43× with drafts 2.7-5.4× faster on separate A100s, Baseten 1.5-2.5× on GPU serving — H7's ≤ 1.3× on the A1 sits at Eq. 2's break-even.
- "Spare FLOPs" on a CPU: OSD's "under 1 percent" utilization is idle GPU; the A1's bf16 matmul is 2 GFLOPS and its fp32 0.5B forward ~90 tok/s (D1) — training there competes for the bus the kids decode on.
- Moving an EAGLE-3/MTP head off the target's box: it needs the target's hidden states (layers [1,15,28]) every draft step; for a 0.6B target the tied LM head (156 MB) is 10× the head layer.
