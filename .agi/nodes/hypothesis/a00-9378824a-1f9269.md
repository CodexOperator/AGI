## Definition (from the brief, applied literally)
A candidate **message daemon** = PERSISTENT (a `watch`/`serve` loop, or a
systemd service with `restart: always`) **AND** MESSAGE-ROUTING (its script
reads an inbox and forwards/relays). A cron job or service that merely polls or
nudges is **not** a daemon. This distinction is asserted, not assumed, in
`test_poll_and_nudge_cron_jobs_are_not_daemons_but_a_service_would_be`.

First version. Two design choices worth recording, because the near-miss for
each is plausible. (1) The gate reads the live node through
`crons.load_crons_node` rather than re-parsing YAML, so "exhaustive
enumeration" is the same enumeration `crons.py apply` performs — a hand-rolled
reader would satisfy the words and drift from production. (2) The routing
signal is NAME-based (a small `ROUTING_SCRIPTS` set plus router stem tokens),
not source-text based, after a source heuristic false-positived on docstrings:
`heal.py` mentions "deliver" in prose and `rotate.py` mentions
inbox/forward/relay, so a text scan flagged BOTH live services as routers on
the first run. The near-miss was to keep the source scan and loosen the tokens
until the suite went green — which would have certified an absence with a gate
biased toward the answer. Name-based is narrower (a router named `worker.py`
would slip past) and that limit is stated rather than hidden.
