## Hypothesis

`rotate.py` can be held to "orchestration only" by a **general** source gate,
not a two-name grep. The gate scans the file with `ast` and fails, naming the
offender, when either appears:

1. a function whose name is argv-shaped (`*_command` / `*_argv` / `*_args`)
   and is not the sole seam `_build_harness_command` or an allowlisted
   non-harness helper;
2. a `harness == "<id>"` comparison against an id outside the shipped set
   (`claude-code`, `copilot-cli`);
3. a second `harness_template.render(...)` call outside the seam.

**Proved if:** the gate reports zero offenders on the live `rotate.py`, AND
synthetic sources containing `_build_grok_command`, `_build_grok_argv`,
`_grok_command`, `if harness == "grok":`, or a second render seam are each
flagged by name.

**Disproved if:** the gate misses any of those, or false-positives on the
allowlisted helpers (`_successor_command`, `_assembled_successor_command`,
`_launch_wrapper_argv`, `_wm_tool_argv`, `_ack_call_args`,
`_run_after_join_command`, `_fork_resume_command`).

## Why the narrow test was not enough

The retired `test_no_named_harness_builders_in_rotate_source` asserted two
hard-coded substrings, `_build_claude_command` and `_build_copilot_command`.
It would have let `_build_grok_command`, `_build_grok_argv`, `_grok_command`,
or `if harness == "grok":` through — precisely the drift a FOURTH harness
invites (`goal:g7.31.2.3`). The general gate closes that hole.
