## Hypothesis

What is the testable claim? What would prove it? What would disprove it?

<!-- THOUGHT:BEGIN — authored, not derived; carried across regenerating scans. The reasoning behind THIS version. -->
THE ACTOR STRING WAS NEVER THE RIGHT THING TO COMPARE, and the reason is structural rather than stylistic. The Prime writes as `belam-S1-L4-II` today and `belam-S1-L4-III` after its next rotation; a gate keyed on that string is a gate that breaks on a schedule nobody controls. `written_by` admits ROLES, and the actor is provenance -- two different jobs that happened to share a field because the only declared type so far was `[moral]`, whose admitted writer `owner` is spelled the same as the actor anyone passes for it. That coincidence is the whole reason the bug survived: the one case in production is the one case where actor and role are the same word.

THE PREFIX RULE IS WHERE THIS ROUND CAN GO QUIETLY WRONG, so the claim spends most of its length there. A naive `startswith` has two failure directions and they fail in OPPOSITE and equally bad ways. If the row name is tested as a prefix without a separator boundary, the row `alive` swallows an actor `aliveness-bot` -- a BYPASS, granting a role nobody assigned. If the comparison is written the other way round, the actor `sanctuary` matches `sanctuary-director`, `sanctuary-helper` AND `sanctuary-master` at once -- ambiguity resolved by whichever row the loop happened to reach first, which is a LOCKOUT or a bypass depending on the dictionary order of a YAML file. Neither announces itself. So the claim demands the boundary, demands longest-wins, and demands that a tie REFUSE rather than pick -- on a fail-closed gate, refusing is the only answer that cannot be silently wrong.

WHY FIXTURES AND NO REAL SCHEMA. The `[config]`/`[vision]` flip to `[owner, prime_director]` is the NEXT round and this one must not front-run it: if the resolver lands with the flip in the same commit, a failure cannot be attributed to either. `[moral]` stays byte-identical for a stronger reason -- it is the one type whose gate is live, and the round that rewrites the resolver underneath it is exactly the round that should prove it still refuses.

ONE THING I ADDED BEYOND THE RULING, flagged so a verdict writer can weigh it: the ruling says 'the row whose name is the actor's seat prefix' and I have required the separator boundary. It is an interpretation, not a quotation. I think a bare prefix is a latent bypass and that the ruling meant a seat name rather than a character sequence, but if the kid finds a live actor that the boundary rule would lock out, that is a finding and it should say so instead of quietly dropping the boundary.
