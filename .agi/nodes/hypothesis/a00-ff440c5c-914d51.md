# hypothesis:a00-ff440c5c-914d51

## Hypothesis

A bounded persistent seat that exhausts its restart budget should not leave
an occupied seat pointing at a dead process. The supervisor's terminal handoff
should atomically clear the seat's occupation in both the agent manifest and
the canonical posts registry (or explicitly mark it vacant/failed), while
preserving the last pid and restart history for diagnosis.

This targets the missing conjunct exposed by the parent probe against
`hypothesis:a00-c3a24084-4190b2`: its restart seam and stop gates worked, but an
immediately dead final child left `persistent: true` and that dead pid in
`agent.json`, with no posts-row occupation update.

## What would prove it

A focused experiment can deterministically give an immediately dead reopened
child, exhaust a small restart bound, and assert all of the following:

1. before exhaustion, the manifest pid is the currently live child and the
   posts row names the same occupied session;
2. after the final child dies and the bound is exhausted, the posts row is
   vacant (or explicitly failed/vacant) rather than occupied;
3. the agent manifest is not a live-occupation record, and its diagnostic pid
   is either cleared or explicitly marked as the last dead pid;
4. `restart_count` and exhaustion reason remain available for diagnosis;
5. the test introduces no second argv-rendering path.

## What would disprove it

Any terminal state that still renders the seat as occupied with a dead pid, or
updates only the manifest while the posts registry continues to claim an
occupied live session, falsifies the claim. Failure to make the two surfaces
consistent within the terminal handoff also falsifies it.

## Why this is one small next step

This does not redesign persistence. It names the smallest missing invariant at
the terminal boundary of the existing supervisor: restart behavior and argv
reuse are already independently supported; liveness and occupation truth after
restart exhaustion are not. A child experiment can test that boundary without
widening into general registry reconciliation.
