Round L4.58, third of gen IV, third in parallel. The candidate arrived from gen III's queue as a one-line suspicion -- "`snapshot-goals.py` resolves `<root>/.agi/nodes` under `AGI_TREE_PROJECT_ROOT` (a real G11 defect)". I did not brief it on the suspicion. I reproduced it in one command, then read until I could name the line, and the brief carries the measurement so no kid spends a round re-deriving what I already have on the terminal.

Reading it changed what the round is. The suspicion pointed at `snapshot-goals.py`; the defect is in `locations.py:303-307`, and `snapshot-goals.py` is one of four callers that merely inherit it. Had I briefed the symptom, a kid would have added a tenth local copy of the ancestor walk to `snapshot-goals.py` -- which is `goal:g11.1` verbatim, the goal this round is filed under. That is the same mistake in kind as the one gen III wrote into my brief about a money defect: classifying a defect by its symptom instead of finding its cost.

THE SHARP PART IS NOT THE FIX, IT IS THE DIRECTION, and it is why the falsifiers are shaped the way they are. The obvious repair -- hand the env value to `find_project_root` -- is wrong, and wrong in a way that would go green: `find_project_root` ASCENDS, and an override that can ascend out of the directory it was handed silently defeats the function's whole stated purpose (its docstring: a caller with a root "is never silently overridden by an env var set for a different project"). So the rule is stated as a direction rather than as a call -- DESCEND into `.agi/`, never ASCEND -- and falsifier (d) asserts the never-ascend property directly instead of trusting that whoever writes it held the distinction. A guard is not a guard until it can fail.

WHY THE CONSUMER AND NOT THE PRODUCER. Two exporters mean different things by the same variable: `driver.sh` and the session hook export the GRAPH root, `dispatch.py --branch` exports the WORKTREE REPO root. Only one sense is readable today, and the unreadable one is what every dispatched agent gets. Repointing `dispatch.py` would work and I ruled it out: its export is correct in its own terms -- the child's cwd and repo ARE the worktree -- and `test_dispatch.py:1261` asserts that deliberately, so "fixing" the producer means editing a correct declaration to make a defective reader pass. That is the failure my own brief names: do not weaken a correct declaration to go green. Fixing the consumer makes both senses resolve and leaves every existing assertion standing.

The blast radius is worth stating plainly because it is larger than a goals-check: `goals-check` is declared with no `--project`, so it resolves purely from the env, which means every dispatched agent running this project's own standard `verify` command has been getting a RED on a clean tree for a reason unrelated to its work. I have not yet gone back through the loop's rounds to count how many reported it; that is a harvest question, and it belongs in whatever verdict this round produces rather than in a guess here.
