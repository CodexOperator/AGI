## CLAIM
Byte-neuron (every kid implements it independently from this spec; three agreeing implementations are part of the evidence): m_i uint8; per tick m <- ((m*205)>>8) + I_i + kick_i + n_i (205/256 = 0.8008 = E3 beta 0.8; n_i uniform in {-1,0,+1}); spike s_i = carry-out (m >= 256); reset = wrap (m & 0xFF = E3 'subtract' at thr 256); flip f_i = s_i XOR s_i[t-1] (e3_lut.py L32). DC drive I_i = round(N(80, sigma_I)) clipped [56,120] (natural periods 3-9 ticks, the E3 DC-arm scale). Synchronous ticks; every neuron reads flips of t-1.
Coupling: all-flip mean field kick_i = floor(K_int * F[t-1] / N), F = popcount of the N-bit flip vector; nearest-flip byte (survey L51's spatial byte) kick_i = floor(K_int * popcount(byte_i[t-1]) / 8), byte_i = flip bits of ring neighbours i+-1..i+-4; spike-mode control uses s in place of f. K = K_int/256 threshold units; grid K_int = 0..256 step 8 (33 points); 4096 ticks, R averaged over the last 2048; seeds {0..4} (drives, initial m, jitter).
Order parameter: theta_i = 2*pi*(t - t_last_i)/(t_last_i - t_prev_i) from the last two carry times; a neuron with t - t_last > 3 periods is excluded; R undefined if < N/2 remain (quiescent); a window with mean rate >= 0.98 is the degenerate all-fire fixed point (R=1 trivially), never 'synchronized'. Information column: R_m = |mean e^{2 pi i m/256}| (a 256-entry LUT on the byte; pilot: does not track R, not claimed).
TESTABLE CLAIM: for N=256, sigma_I=10, all-flip coupling, R(K) crosses 0.5 at a K_c whose 5-seed CV is <= 5%, with sharpness K_c/[K(R=0.75)-K(R=0.25)] >= 2, R(0) <= 0.125 (= 2/sqrt(N)) and R(K_int=192) >= 0.9 at mean rate <= 0.9 spikes/tick. Sharpness >= 2 separates a switch from a dimmer: a linear ramp 0.08 -> 0.99 over the grid scores ~0.8.

## FALSIFIERS

- K_c CV > 15% over 5 seeds, or R(0) > 0.125 -> the switch point is not a design constant, or the K=0 state is not asynchronous; a gate would need per-instance calibration. Sharpness < 1 -> a dimmer, not a switch.
- Kid B: K_c(ring-8)/K_c(all-flip) > 1.25 or R(192) < 0.5 on the ring -> the local byte cannot reach the switch; only the global popcount (N/8 bytes/tick) can, and 'nearest-flip coupling' is dead as wiring.
